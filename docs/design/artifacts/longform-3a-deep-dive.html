CHAPTER ONE
The contract is the asset

A desalination company does not sell water. It sells availability. Under a standard Gulf water purchase agreement — and there are only really three templates across the region — the offtaker pays a fixed capacity charge every month the plant is available to produce, whether or not a single cubic metre is drawn, plus a variable charge that passes fuel and chemicals straight through at cost.

Which means the revenue line of a company like ACWA Power's water arm has more in common with a leased office tower than with a utility. Demand risk sits with the government. Fuel risk is passed through. What is left for the equity holder is operational risk — can you keep the membranes running — and that is a risk these operators have spent fifteen years learning to price.

Offtaker bears demand. Equity bears availability. A plant at 97% availability collects 100% of the charge; below 95% liquidated damages begin.
FUEL & CHEMICALS
Passed through at cost with a 30-day lag. No margin, no exposure — but it inflates reported revenue and flatters the top line in energy-price spikes.

CHAPTER TWO
Two markets, one asset, 180 basis points apart

Here is the anomaly the whole piece turns on. The project bonds issued against these plants trade at spreads that imply an investment-grade, quasi-sovereign contracted cash flow. The listed equity of the same operators trades on an implied cost of equity that assumes something considerably riskier.

"The bondholders and the shareholders are looking at the same 25-year contract and disagreeing by 180 basis points about what it is worth."

CHAPTER FOUR
The 2028 membrane cliff

Reverse-osmosis membranes last seven to nine years. The Gulf's build-out clustered heavily in 2019–2021, which means a replacement wave lands between 2027 and 2029 — inside most investors' holding periods and outside most models.

Our plant-level database puts the aggregate replacement bill at $2.4bn across the 61 plants, of which roughly 61% is recoverable through the capacity charge under the standard WPA and 39% is not. The distribution is what matters: three operators carry two-thirds of the unrecoverable portion, and one of them is trading at a premium to the group…

Chapter four works through the schedule plant by plant, then reprices all six names on the corrected free-cash-flow profile.
