The most expensive words in Gulf banking this year have been "higher for longer." Twelve months of them convinced the market that Saudi deposit costs would never normalise, that time deposits would keep cannibalising CASA, and that the sector's net interest margin — 2.84% at the Q1 trough on our tracker — was the new permanent state of affairs.

The data has stopped cooperating with that story. Across the ten banks we cover, the share of non-interest-bearing deposits rose 110 bp quarter-on-quarter in Q2, while SAIBOR's drift below 4.8% has quietly repriced the marginal time deposit. Funding-cost relief arrives two quarters before consensus models say it should.

"Consensus is paying trough multiples for mid-cycle margins. That gap is the whole trade."

Who benefits first is a function of retail-repricing beta, and the dispersion is wide. Al Rajhi carries a 68% CASA share and a mortgage book that reprices faster than any peer's corporate ledger. Alinma runs it close on deposit momentum with the added kicker of a smaller, cleaner balance sheet.
