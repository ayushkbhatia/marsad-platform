What actually happens on an ex-dividend date

Every quarter, thousands of GCC investors buy a stock the morning it goes ex-dividend and are surprised not to receive the payment. Here is the mechanism, in order, with the dates that matter and a worked example on a real Tadawul name.
THE TIMELINE Four dates, in the order they occur SAUDI ARAMCO · Q2 2026 INTERIM · SAR 0.2043
1 · DECLARATION
5 JUL
The board announces the amount and the dates. Nothing changes in your account; the share price may move on the news.
2 · EX-DATE
12 JUL
The deadline that catches people. Buy on or after this morning's open and the dividend goes to the seller, not to you. The quoted price typically opens lower by roughly the dividend.
3 · RECORD DATE
13 JUL
The registrar takes a snapshot of who owns the shares. Because settlement is T+2, this falls after the ex-date — which is why the ex-date exists.
4 · PAY DATE
25 JUL
Cash lands in the account of whoever appeared on the register. Roughly two weeks after the ex-date on Tadawul; varies by venue.
THE ONE-LINE VERSION To receive the dividend you must own the shares before the ex-date opens — which in practice means buying at least one full trading day earlier.
Why the price drops on the ex-date

A company about to pay out cash is worth more than the same company immediately after. On the ex-date the exchange adjusts the reference price down by the dividend amount, so the opening quote is mechanically lower. This is not the market falling — it is arithmetic.

Your total position is unchanged at that instant: you hold a share worth slightly less plus a receivable worth the dividend. Whether the price then recovers the gap is a separate question, and it depends on the market, not on the mechanism.

Three ways this trips people up
01
"The stock fell and I don't know why"
Check whether it went ex that morning. A 5% yielder drops ~5% on its annual ex-date and nothing has gone wrong. Marsad flags ex-dates on the price chart for exactly this reason.
02
Buying on the ex-date to "capture" the dividend
Too late by one day. The register snapshot happens the following day but settlement of your purchase does not clear in time.
03
Assuming a bonus issue works the same way
It doesn't. A 5% bonus issue adjusts the price down about 4.8% and gives you more shares instead of cash — the screen looks cheaper overnight and the position is unchanged. Separate explainer →
WHAT THIS IS NOT
Nothing here is a suggestion to buy or sell around an ex-date. "Dividend capture" strategies are widely discussed and rarely survive costs and taxes. This explainer describes a mechanism so you can read your own account statement — that is all.

Terms on this page
Ex-date
The first trading day on which a buyer is not entitled to the declared dividend.
Record date
The day the registrar photographs the share register to decide who gets paid.
T+2 settlement
A trade legally completes two business days after execution — the reason ex- and record dates differ.
Payout ratio
Dividends as a share of earnings. Above 100% the company is distributing more than it earns.
